Abstract

Generative posterior sampling using diffusion models has emerged as a dominant paradigm for solving inverse problems in imaging, which usually consists of three main components: data consistency (DC) guidance, classifier-free guidance (CFG) and stochasticity. While prior arts have focused on how to develop each or all components, less attention has been given to how to schedule them, leading to heuristically fixed or partially adjusted suboptimal schedules. In this work, we argue that the interactions among all three components in terms of scheduling are crucial for significantly improved performance. Our analysis shows that aggressive CFG early in sampling conflicts with DC guidance, while stochasticity brings the trajectory back to higher-probability regions. Based on these findings, we propose Triadic Dynamics Aware Posterior Sampling (TriPS), which reformulates posterior sampling as a time-varying control problem and optimizes schedules following a triadic trend of decreasing DC and stochasticity scales alongside increasing CFG scale. TriPS achieves this through two strategies: template-based search over functional priors for reliable baseline schedules, and GRPO-based reinforcement learning for more flexible temporal curves. Experiments demonstrate TriPS outperforms state-of-the-art baselines in data fidelity and perceptual realism.

Key idea

A coupled triad, scheduled jointly

Posterior sampling is driven by three forces. Treating their schedules — not just their values — as the object of optimization unlocks a markedly better perception–distortion trade-off.

Data consistency · β(t) ↓

Strong early on to lock global structure to the measurement, then annealed to avoid over-enforcing and injecting measurement noise.

CFG · λ(t) ↑

Kept low early — aggressive CFG conflicts with DC guidance — then raised late to sharpen semantics once structure is set.

Stochasticity · η(t) ↓

High early to regularize off-manifold drift back to higher-probability regions, then annealed to reduce late-stage sampling error.

Method

Triadic schedule optimization

TriPST · Template search

Each schedule is one of {linear, exponential, logarithmic}, with magnitudes bounded (λ∈[1,6], η∈[0,1], β∈[βmin,βmax]). A grid search over 𝒯³ maximizes a fidelity–perception utility 𝒰(τ) on a small calibration set, giving a robust baseline and a warm-start for GRPO.

TriPSG · GRPO optimization

Schedules are Bernstein polynomials whose coefficients are sampled from Beta distributions (convex-hull + partition-of-unity ⇒ always in range). The policy is optimized by a clipped GRPO objective with a KL anchor to the TriPST reference, driven by a hybrid reward (PSNR + LPIPS / CLIP-IQA+ / Q-Align).
