Key Idea

Schedule the triad, not just its values

Diffusion posterior sampling for inverse problems is driven by three components: data consistency (DC) guidance, classifier-free guidance (CFG), and stochasticity (STO). Prior works mostly tune how strong each component is, yet leave how they evolve over time fixed by heuristics. TriPS instead casts posterior sampling as a time-varying control problem and jointly optimizes the three schedules.

DC guidance · β(t) ↓

Strong early on to lock global structure onto the measurement, then annealed so that over enforcing consistency does not inject measurement noise into the result.

CFG · λ(t) ↑

Kept low early to avoid the conflict with DC guidance, then raised once the global structure is settled so that semantics are sharpened in the low noise regime.

Stochasticity · η(t) ↓

High early to regularize the off manifold drift caused by DC and CFG, then annealed to reduce late stage sampling error and to tighten the match to the target distribution.

Analysis

Triadic Coupling Dynamics

We characterize DC guidance, CFG, and stochasticity as one interacting dynamical system, which exposes why a carefully ordered schedule is needed rather than independently tuned constants.

Method

Triadic Schedule Optimization

TriPST · Template based search

Each component is one of {linear, exponential, logarithmic} with bounded magnitudes (λ in [1, 6], η in [0, 1], β in [βmin, βmax]). A grid search over the template triple maximizes a fidelity and perception utility on a small calibration set, giving a robust baseline and a warm start for GRPO.

TriPSG · GRPO optimization

Schedules are Bernstein polynomials whose coefficients are sampled from Beta distributions, so the convex hull and partition of unity keep every curve in range. A clipped GRPO objective with a KL anchor to the TriPST reference is driven by a hybrid reward (PSNR with LPIPS, CLIP-IQA+, and Q-Align).
